# SEO Audit: Sociedad de Astronomía del Caribe

**Repo root:** `c:\Users\abdie\Documents\sac\sac-website\` (all file paths below are relative to this root)
**Live production host:** `https://www.sociedadastronomia.com` (apex 308-redirects to www, verified live)
**Corpus:** 77 published articles in S3 (`sac-blog-articles`), 13 drafts, 1 archived
**Audit date:** 2026-08-23

---

## 1. Verdict

The articles **can** rank today, and some of them almost certainly do. Pages are genuinely server-rendered, `index, follow` is set everywhere, `<html lang="es">` is correct, every article carries one `<h1>`, valid `Article` JSON-LD, OpenGraph and Twitter cards, and all 77 legacy URLs survived the S3 migration intact. What is broken is **discovery and identity**: the production sitemap contains exactly one URL (`/gallery`, on the wrong hostname), no page on the site emits a `rel=canonical`, and every machine-readable URL the site publishes about itself (og:url, JSON-LD `url`, all 77 RSS `<guid>`/`<link>` values, the sitemap `<loc>`) names `https://sac-website.vercel.app//...` with the wrong host and a double slash, while that preview host serves a byte-identical, fully indexable copy of the entire site.

So the site is not blocked, it is **mis-addressed and undiscoverable in depth**. New articles depend on Googlebot walking a prev/next-only chain up to 16 pages deep, the 13 newest articles have no tags and therefore no hub links at all, and only 2 internal links exist across the entire 77-article body corpus. One unknown outranks everything if it turns out badly: Vercel's firewall challenges every non-browser HTTP client with a 429, and whether real Googlebot is exempted must be confirmed in Search Console before anything else is prioritized.

## 6. Strategic content and keyword recommendations

These are grounded in the real corpus, not generic advice. The organisation's one defensible advantage is that it is the local astronomical authority for Puerto Rico. 24 of 77 titles and 29 of 77 summaries already carry that signal, and they correlate with the strongest pages. The other 54 titles compete head-on with NASA and global wire copy and will lose.

**1. Build recurring-event landing pages that persist, instead of one-off news posts.**
The corpus already covers the same events every cycle: "Intenso eclipse lunar en la noche del 27 al 28 de agosto de 2026", "Eclipse Total del Sol el lunes 8 de Abril, 2024", "Lluvia de meteoros Perseidas", "Se verá desde Puerto Rico el lanzamiento de un cohete que despegará desde la Florida". Each is published, ranks for two weeks, then dies. Create four evergreen hubs that are updated rather than replaced: *Eclipses visibles desde Puerto Rico*, *Lluvias de meteoros: calendario para Puerto Rico*, *Lanzamientos visibles desde la Isla*, *Cometas visibles ahora*. Every news post links up to its hub; the hub links down to the current event. This turns 40+ isolated news items into four pages that accumulate authority year over year and hold rankings between events.

**2. Own the "¿qué fue esa luz en el cielo?" query.**
SAC is already the de facto bolide reporting body: "Un bólido se observó tan brillante que se vio a través de las nubes", "Brillante Meteoro avistado en Puerto Rico", "'Se vio enorme, mostrando una cola y un color verdoso', coincidieron varios observadores al reportar el avistamiento a la Sociedad de Astronomía del Caribe". These events produce massive, instantaneous local search spikes in colloquial Spanish. Build a permanent *Reporta un bólido o avistamiento* page with a report form, plus an annual *Bólidos y meteoros reportados en Puerto Rico* roundup. Title future posts in the language people type: "¿Qué fue la luz verde en el cielo de Puerto Rico anoche?" rather than "Brillante meteoro avistado".

**3. Rebuild the telescope guide as the commercial-intent anchor.**
`/blog/telescopios`, "Guía de Recomendaciones de Telescopios", is the one page with recurring purchase intent, it is already in the sitewide nav, and it currently declares `datePublished: 2000-01-01` and sorts last of 77. Fix the date, expand it, retitle toward the query ("Qué telescopio comprar: guía para principiantes en Puerto Rico"), and spin off satellites: *Telescopio para niños*, *Binoculares para astronomía*, *Dónde comprar telescopios en PR*. Link every "cómo verlo" news post to it. This is the highest-yield single page on the site and it is currently the hardest to reach.

**4. Add the local answer block to every viewing article, and put the geography in the title.**
The articles that already win are the ones with a place name: "Desde Isabela, PR: Espectacular foto del Cometa 220P / McNaught", "Cometa A3 desde Isabela, PR por Victor Rivera / SAC". Standardize a three-heading block on every observable-event article: `## ¿A qué hora se verá desde Puerto Rico?` (with AST times), `## ¿Hacia dónde mirar?`, `## ¿Necesito telescopio?`. This simultaneously fixes the missing-subheadings finding, creates featured-snippet targets, and reinforces the geographic signal. Rewrite the worst offenders, for example "Captan 'Eclipse Solar' desde Marte" to "Eclipse solar en Marte: Perseverance capta a Fobos", and "Eclipse Total del Sol el lunes 8 de Abril, 2024" to "Eclipse solar total del 8 de abril de 2024: hora en Puerto Rico".

**5. Ship the observation guides as real pages, because they are the only original data on the site.**
The three guide editions in `s3://sac-blog-articles/guides/` contain roughly 66 deep-sky objects with magnitudes, difficulty ratings, equipment notes and optimal observing times. That is first-party, structured, evergreen, locally-scoped data that no wire service can duplicate, and today it is invisible: three editions share one URL and none of the objects reach the server HTML. Per-edition URLs plus per-object anchors turn this into the site's most durable ranking asset for "qué observar", "galaxias de invierno", "guía de astrofotografía" queries. Rename the editions first: "Febrero 2026" and "Junio 2026" are not titles.
